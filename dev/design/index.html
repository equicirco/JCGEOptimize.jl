Design

Role in JCGE

JCGEOptimize is designed as an optional layer around equilibrium models. A search algorithm proposes candidate decisions, the model computes their equilibrium outcomes, and the optimization layer evaluates the objectives and constraints before proposing further candidates.

The architecture is general: policy design, parameter optimization, and single-objective or multi-objective problems share this separation between candidate decisions and equilibrium calculations.

Responsibilities

The optimization component defines and coordinates:

  • Decision variables, their bounds, and their mapping to model inputs.
  • Objectives and constraints computed from model outcomes.
  • Interchangeable optimization backends: deterministic grid search and an optional Metaheuristics.jl extension.
  • Evaluation records, solver-failure handling, and reproducible search histories.
  • Results representing an optimum candidate or trade-offs among objectives.

The user supplies an evaluator that maps named decisions to a compact outcome. For a JCGE model, this evaluator calls JCGERuntime, checks solve status and residuals, and extracts the quantities needed by the objectives and constraints. JCGEOptimize accepts that outcome through an explicit validity callback. Economic equations and closures remain defined by the model and its JCGE blocks.

Optimization histories are returned as flat named-tuple rows for tabular export. The model application can use JCGEOutput to collect and report individual equilibrium results; the generic search code has no mandatory dependency on a particular model, solver, or reporting package.

Validity, feasibility, and search results

Model validity and policy feasibility are separate. A candidate can satisfy the equilibrium equations but violate a revenue requirement. Conversely, a failed equilibrium cannot qualify for the optimum even if its tentative indicators look attractive. Every attempted model evaluation remains in the history.

Constraint tolerances use the original outcome units. Scales normalize the residuals passed to the backend, while reported objective and constraint values retain their original units. Unexpected evaluator exceptions propagate by default; applications may explicitly choose to record them as failed evaluations. Interrupts always propagate.

Single-objective searches report the best feasible evaluated candidate. Searches with several objectives report feasible nondominated candidates without assigning unstated weights. A heuristic result does not certify global optimality or the uniqueness of the equilibria used to evaluate it.

Backends

GridSearch provides a deterministic comparison over declared axes. MetaheuristicsBackend receives an algorithm factory so applications can select the appropriate single-objective or multi-objective algorithm. The optional extension uses a fixed seed and a hard model-evaluation budget, including the initial point and failed evaluations. Candidate evaluation is currently serial.

Algorithms receive finite penalties for rejected evaluations. Infinities can produce undefined arithmetic in fitness-weighted calculations, including ECA's population centres. failure_penalty defaults to 1e6 in the scaled minimization objectives and constraint residuals passed to the algorithm. Applications can choose another finite positive value appropriate to their normalized objectives and constraints. This is a numerical search setting: failure records retain their original status and unavailable outcomes, and failed candidates are excluded from the reported optimum regardless of the penalty.
